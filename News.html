Top 5 Mistakes to Avoid When Installing SPC Wall Panels

SPC (Stone Polymer Composite) wall panels are designed for fast, clean, and durable installation. But even the best panels can fail if installed incorrectly. Avoiding these five common mistakes will save you time, money, and callbacks.

Mistake #1: Ignoring Wall Flatness and Preparation

The Problem: SPC panels are rigid. If the wall behind them is uneven, the panels will rock, creak, or develop gaps at the seams.

The Fix:

  • Check wall flatness with a 2m straightedge. Maximum deviation should be ≤3mm per 2m.
  • Fill deep holes and sand down high spots.
  • For minor unevenness, use shims or adjustable clips.
  • For very uneven walls, consider installing a batten system first.

Remember: A flat wall is the foundation of a flawless installation.

Mistake #2: Installing Without Expansion Gaps

The Problem: SPC panels expand and contract with temperature changes. If installed tight against walls, ceilings, or fixed objects, they can buckle, warp, or crack.

The Fix:

  • Leave a 5–8mm expansion gap around all perimeters.
  • Use skirting mouldings, ceiling lines, and corner trims to cover the gaps neatly.
  • For large installations, leave expansion gaps between sections every 6–8 meters.

Remember: SPC needs room to breathe.

Mistake #3: Using the Wrong Adhesive or Glue

The Problem: Some installers use general-purpose construction adhesives that are not compatible with SPC. This can cause the panel to delaminate, warp, or fail to bond properly.

The Fix:

  • Use PUR hot glue (as specified in our quotation sheet) for tongue-and-groove joints.
  • For click-lock systems, no glue is needed—ensure the profile is clean.
  • Never use solvent-based adhesives that can react with the PVC film.

Remember: Always follow the manufacturer's adhesive recommendation.

Mistake #4: Damaging the Click-Lock or Tongue-and-Groove Profile

The Problem: Forcing panels together, hitting them with a hammer, or not aligning the profile properly can break the joint. This leads to gaps, loose panels, and water ingress.

The Fix:

  • Align the profile carefully before clicking.
  • Use a rubber mallet and a tapping block—never a metal hammer.
  • For tongue-and-groove panels, apply glue evenly and slide panels together gently.
  • If a profile is damaged, do not install it—replace the panel.

Remember: The joint is the weakest point. Protect it.

Mistake #5: Exposing Panels to Extreme Temperatures Before Installation

The Problem: SPC panels stored in freezing cold or scorching hot conditions can become brittle or overly soft. Installing them immediately can cause cracking, warping, or poor bonding.

The Fix:

  • Store panels in the room where they will be installed for at least 48 hours before installation.
  • Ideal installation temperature: 15°C to 30°C.
  • Avoid installing in direct sunlight or near heat sources.

Remember: Acclimation is not optional—it is essential.

SPC & WPC Wall Panels: A Buyer's Guide to Certifications

When sourcing SPC or WPC wall panels, the datasheet often lists a long string of certifications: CE, French A+, Class B, Zero-Formaldehyde, Heavy-Metal Free. But what do these actually mean—and which ones matter for your project?

1. CE Marking: Your Passport to the EU Market

CE is not a quality mark. It is a declaration by the manufacturer that the product meets applicable EU health, safety, and environmental requirements. For construction products, CE marking is usually supported by a Declaration of Performance (DoP) and test reports from an accredited laboratory.

What to check: Ask for the DoP and the underlying test reports—not just a CE logo on the brochure.

2. French A+: Indoor Air Quality

French A+ is the highest class under France's VOC emission labelling scheme. It measures volatile organic compounds released from building products over 28 days. An A+ rating means very low emissions—important for schools, hospitals, offices, and homes.

What to check: Confirm the A+ certificate covers the exact product and surface finish you are buying.

3. Class B Fire Retardant: Reaction to Fire

Class B under EN 13501-1 means the panel makes a "very limited contribution to fire." It is commonly required for commercial interiors, hotels, retail, and public spaces. A full Class B rating includes two additional codes: s (smoke production) and d (flaming droplets). A rating of B-s2, d0 is stronger than B-s3, d0.

What to check: Ask for the full classification, not just "Class B." For high-rise, healthcare, or escape routes, specify B-s2, d0 or better.

4. Zero Formaldehyde Release

Formaldehyde is a common indoor air pollutant found in some adhesives and resins. Zero formaldehyde release means the product does not release formaldehyde under test conditions. This is especially relevant for interior wall panels used in enclosed spaces.

What to check: Request the formaldehyde test report. Look for "not detected" or "zero release" based on a recognised method.

5. Free Heavy Metals Migration

Heavy metals such as lead, cadmium, mercury, and chromium VI can be present in PVC films or fillers. Free heavy metals migration means the product has been tested and meets strict limits for heavy-metal release.

What to check: Ask for the migration test report and confirm it covers the surface film and core material.

What to Ask Your Supplier

  1. Are the certificates issued by a third-party lab such as SGS, TÜV, or Intertek?
  2. Do the certificates match the exact SKU, thickness, and surface finish?
  3. Is the CE marking supported by a Declaration of Performance?
  4. What is the full fire classification—B-s2, d0 or B-s3, d0?
  5. Does the French A+ certificate cover all VOC categories?
  6. Is formaldehyde tested as "not detected" or only "low emission"?
  7. Are heavy-metal migration test reports available?

How to Choose the Right SPC Wall Panel Thickness: 6mm vs 8mm

One of the most common questions we receive from buyers is: "Should I choose 6mm or 8mm SPC wall panels?" Both thicknesses are durable, waterproof, and suitable for interior wall cladding. But they serve different project needs.

When to Choose 6mm SPC Boards

  • High-rise buildings: Lower weight reduces structural load.
  • Retrofit and renovation: Lighter panels are easier to handle.
  • Budget-focused projects: Lower material cost per square meter.
  • Standard residential interiors: Sufficient durability for bedrooms, living rooms, and ceilings.
  • Ceiling applications: Reduced weight is a significant advantage for overhead installation.

Key benefit: At 4.9–5.7 kg/m², 6mm boards are noticeably lighter—easier to transport, handle, and install, especially when working at height.

When to Choose 8mm SPC Boards

  • Commercial interiors: Superior impact resistance for offices, retail, and hospitality.
  • High-traffic areas: Handles daily wear from carts, luggage, and foot traffic.
  • Large-format panels: Available up to 1120mm wide—fewer seams, faster coverage.
  • V-Seam and U-Seam designs: Full range of seam profiles for architectural detailing.
  • Feature walls: Rigid core holds shape on tall walls without bowing.
  • Hotels, hospitals, schools: Meets commercial durability and fire safety requirements.

Key benefit: At 6.2–7.4 kg/m², 8mm boards provide a solid, premium feel with significantly higher rigidity. The wider range of widths (up to 1120mm) means fewer panels per project and fewer seams.

When specifying wall finishes for a residential or commercial project, the choice often comes down to two options: traditional ceramic tiles or modern SPC wall boards. Both have their place—but they differ dramatically in cost, installation speed, and long-term performance.

Cost: Material vs. Total Installed Cost

The biggest mistake buyers make is comparing only the material price per square meter. The real number that matters is the total installed cost.

Cost ComponentCeramic TilesSPC Wall Boards
Material CostLow to highMid-range, stable

Class B Fire-Retardant Wall Panels: Standards Explained

If you are sourcing wall panels for a commercial project, you have likely seen terms like "Class B," "B1," "B-s2, d0," or "EN 13501-1" on product datasheets. These codes are not interchangeable—and understanding them can mean the difference between passing or failing a building inspection.

The European Standard: EN 13501-1

In Europe and most international markets, fire performance for building products is classified under EN 13501-1. This standard ranks materials into seven classes based on how they react to fire:

ClassFire Performance
A1Non-combustible (highest)
A2Limited combustibility
BVery limited contribution to fire
CLimited contribution to fire
DModerate contribution to fire
EHigh contribution to fire
FNo performance determined (lowest)

Class B is the rating most commercial interiors require—it means the material contributes very little to fire growth.

Two Tests Behind Every Class B Rating

To earn a Class B classification, a product must pass two separate fire tests:

1. Single Burning Item (SBI) Test — EN 13823
A sample is exposed to a burning object in a corner configuration for 20 minutes. The test measures:

ParameterWhat It Measures
FIGRAFire Growth Rate Index — how fast fire spreads
THR600sTotal Heat Release in the first 600 seconds
SMOGRASmoke Growth Rate Index
TSP600sTotal Smoke Production in 600 seconds
LFSWhether flames reach the edge of the sample

2. Single-Flame Source Test — EN ISO 11925-2
A small flame is applied directly to the surface for 30 seconds. The test measures whether the material ignites and whether the flame spreads beyond 150mm.

Understanding the Full Classification Code

A Class B rating is always followed by two additional codes:

B – s [smoke] , d [droplets]

Smoke Production (s): s1 = Lowest smoke production, s2 = Moderate, s3 = Highest.
Flaming Droplets (d): d0 = No flaming droplets within 600 seconds (best), d1 = No droplets persisting longer than 10 seconds, d2 = Neither d0 nor d1 (worst).

So a rating of B-s2, d0 means: Class B fire performance, moderate smoke, and no flaming droplets.

What About "Class B1"?

Class B1 is not the same as Class B. B1 is a German standard (DIN 4102), while Class B is under EN 13501-1. Some suppliers still use the German B1 rating, but it is being phased out. If a datasheet says "B1," ask for the EN 13501-1 equivalent.

SPC Boards: Honeycomb Core vs. Square Hole Core – A Complete Guide

When choosing SPC wall panels, most buyers focus on surface finish, color, and thickness. But the core structure inside the board matters just as much—it affects weight, strength, installation, and cost. Two of the most common core designs are Honeycomb Core and Square Hole Core.

What Is a Square Hole Core?

A Square Hole Core SPC board features a series of square or rectangular cavities running through the interior. This hollow-core design reduces weight while maintaining structural rigidity.

  • Thickness: 6mm and 8mm
  • Density: Approximately 6.2–7.0 kg/m² (8mm board)
  • Structure: Uniform, parallel square chambers
  • Best for: Standard residential and commercial wall cladding where cost efficiency and reliable performance are the priority.

What Is a Honeycomb Core?

A Honeycomb Core SPC board uses a hexagonal cell pattern—inspired by natural beehive structures—inside the panel. This design delivers a very high strength-to-weight ratio, similar to aerospace and automotive engineering principles.

  • Thickness: 6mm and 8mm
  • Density: Lighter than square hole core
  • Structure: Hexagonal honeycomb chambers
  • Best for: Large-format panels, projects where reduced structural load matters, and premium applications requiring higher rigidity with less weight.

Head-to-Head Comparison

FeatureSquare Hole CoreHoneycomb Core
StructureSquare/rectangular chambersHexagonal honeycomb cells
WeightStandard (heavier)Lighter
Strength-to-Weight RatioGoodExcellent
RigidityHighVery High
Surface FlatnessExcellentExcellent
Acoustic PerformanceGoodBetter
Thermal InsulationModerateGood
CostMore cost-effectiveHigher investment
Best ApplicationResidential, retail, standard commercialLarge-format, premium, weight-sensitive projects

Which One Should You Choose?

Choose Square Hole Core if:

  • You need a cost-effective solution for standard wall cladding
  • Your project uses 6mm or 8mm boards in common widths (150mm–600mm)
  • You want reliable, proven performance for residential or retail interiors

Choose Honeycomb Core if:

  • You need larger panel formats with fewer seams
  • Weight reduction matters—for example, in high-rise buildings or retrofit projects
  • You want superior acoustic performance for hotels, offices, or conference rooms
  • The project specification calls for premium structural performance
